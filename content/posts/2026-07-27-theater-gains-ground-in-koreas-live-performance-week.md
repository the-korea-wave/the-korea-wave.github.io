Plays widened their footprint in South Korea's live-performance market during the last full week of July 2026, climbing from 8 percent to 12 percent of ranked box-office activity — a four-percentage-point gain that stands out in an otherwise concert-dominated week. The shift, drawn from the KOPIS box-office ranking of 50 productions, points to more theater titles reaching audiences across the country, though none broke into the week's top tier.

## Where the four points came from

Across the 50 ranked productions, six were straight plays, up from the prior week's share and enough to lift theater to 12 percent of the chart. The rest of the field skewed heavily toward music: 23 popular-music productions, 17 musicals, two Western classical concerts, one multi-genre program, and one circus-and-magic act. In that mix, theater's rise is a story of breadth rather than blockbusters — more plays earning a place on the board, spread across a schedule that music still leads by a wide margin.

## K-pop keeps the summit

The top of the ranking told a different story from the one unfolding lower down. Popular music took seven of the ten highest-placed productions, led by NCT 127's 5TH TOUR, NEO CITY: THE REDLINE in Seoul at number one, followed by Young K's solo tour YOUNGEST in Incheon at three, Sung Si-kyung's "with friends" run at four, the Busan International Rock Festival at five, CORTIS's PUT YOUR PHONE DOWN birthday-party tour at six, izna's WHO DAT GIRL? tour at eight, and Benson Boone's Gyeonggi date at nine.

Musicals filled the remaining three slots, with Dracula in Seoul at number two, Beethoven at seven, and The Brothers Karamazov in Daehangno at ten. No play appeared in the top ten, underscoring that theater's expansion is happening in the middle and lower reaches of the chart rather than at its peak.

## A high-turnover week

Churn ran heavy: 29 of the 50 ranked productions were new entries — more than half the board turning over in a single week. Three of those debuts landed near the top, including the chart-leading NCT 127 tour, Young K's Incheon concert, and Sung Si-kyung's Seoul dates. Among returning productions, 11 climbed in rank while 10 slipped, leaving movement roughly balanced beneath the surface of the new arrivals.

## Concentration ticked up even as variety widened

The week carried a quiet tension. Even as theater broadened its presence, the leading genre tightened its grip: the top genre's share of ranked activity edged from 44 percent to 46 percent, tracking popular music's 23 of 50 productions. In other words, more play titles reached the chart while concert programming pulled a larger slice of the total — expansion at the edges, concentration at the core.

Geography reinforced the capital's pull. Seven of the top ten productions played Seoul, with Incheon, Busan, and Gyeonggi accounting for one apiece — a reminder that even a diversifying week still routes most of its marquee demand through the metropolitan center.
