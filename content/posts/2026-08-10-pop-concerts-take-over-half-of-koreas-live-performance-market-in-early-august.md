Pop music now accounts for more than half of Korea's live performance market. In the week spanning early August 2026, the genre's share of the KOPIS box office rankings climbed from 42% to 52%, a ten-percentage-point jump in a single week that pushed concerts past musicals, classical music, and theater combined. The surge coincided with an unusually heavy slate of 32 new productions entering the chart, headlined by three major concert launches: AKMU's "Paradise of Rumors," BIGBANG's world tour date in Goyang, and fromis_9's Asia tour opener in Seoul.

## A Top 10 Dominated by Concert Stages

The weekly top 10 makes the shift concrete. Seven of the ten highest-grossing productions were pop music events, and the podium belonged entirely to the genre: AKMU's "Paradise of Rumors" in Seoul took first place, BIGBANG's stadium stop in Goyang ranked second, and fromis_9's "TOMORROW GLOW." Seoul date came third. They were followed by the Xnterstellar Music Festival in Incheon at fourth and Lim Young-woong's "IM HERO, THE STADIUM 2" in Gyeonggi Province at fifth — meaning concerts swept the entire top five.

The remaining pop entries underscore the genre's generational breadth. Lee Seung-chul's 40th-anniversary "THE VOICE" concert in Seoul placed eighth, and CNBLUE's "3LOGY" encore in Busan came ninth — veteran soloists and second-generation bands charting alongside current idol acts and a music festival.

## Musicals Hold Their Ground, Classical Squeezes In

Musicals remained the second-largest category with 17 of the 50 ranked productions, though only two cracked the top 10: "Dracula" at sixth and "Dear Evan Hansen" at seventh, both running in Seoul. Classical music managed a single top-10 appearance — the Charles Dutoit and Martha Argerich program with the KBS Symphony Orchestra, at tenth — out of just two ranked classical productions overall. Theater also fielded two entries, while dance, interdisciplinary work, and circus and magic contributed one each.

That distribution — 26 pop productions against 24 for every other genre combined — is what drove the concentration figure. The top genre's share of the market moved from 42% to 52% week-over-week, matching the pop share itself: the market's center of gravity and its concentration are now the same thing.

## Heavy Churn Beneath the Rankings

The week was also notable for turnover. Alongside the 32 new entrants, twelve productions fell in the rankings against only six that rose — a ratio suggesting the incoming concert wave displaced incumbents rather than expanding the chart around them. With 50 productions ranked in total, nearly two-thirds of the box office list was either brand new or losing ground.

Whether the 52% concentration holds depends largely on scheduling. Stadium-scale tours like BIGBANG's and Lim Young-woong's run on fixed date blocks, so the genre's share could recede as quickly as it expanded once those runs close. For now, though, the KOPIS data describe a market where the summer concert calendar — not the long-running musical repertoire — is setting the terms.
