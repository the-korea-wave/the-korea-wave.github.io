South Korea's box office contracted sharply in the last full week of August, with total weekly revenue falling 14.8% from the previous frame, according to data from the Korean Film Council's KOBIS ticketing system. The decline came despite a still-commanding number one: Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey took in ₩28.5 billion from roughly 2.57 million admissions, pushing its cumulative Korean audience past 7.1 million.

## A Market Running on One Engine

The week's numbers describe a market leaning heavily on a single title. The Odyssey's ₩28.5 billion haul was more than three times the ₩8.8 billion earned by second-place Spider-Man: Brand New Day, which drew 848,282 admissions and lifted its cumulative total to 8.13 million — still the larger overall run, but now clearly past its peak weeks.

Concentration at the top remains extreme even as it edged down. The three highest-grossing films accounted for 87.5% of revenue among the leaders, compared with 88.5% a week earlier. That one-point easing did little to change the underlying picture: outside the top two, no film cleared ₩1.5 billion for the week.

## Family Animation Fills the Middle of the Chart

The mid-table belonged almost entirely to animated and family titles. Heartsping: The Legend of the Whale Jewel, the latest entry in the homegrown Teenieping franchise, held third with ₩1.46 billion from 154,738 admissions, bringing its cumulative audience to 788,263. Detective Conan's new theatrical feature followed in fourth with ₩1.32 billion, while Paw Patrol: The Dino Movie sat seventh, and Studio Ghibli's The Secret World of Arrietty re-entered the chart in ninth on a re-release, adding 17,436 admissions to a cumulative count of nearly 1.09 million.

The Korean comedy sequel Okay! Madam 2 ranked fifth on 101,544 admissions, though its ₩968 million in revenue actually trailed the sixth-place film — a reminder that KOBIS orders its weekly chart by attendance, and that discounted or matinee-heavy audiences can separate the two measures.

## Horror Arrives, but Only Two Films Are New

Just two titles entered the top ten. The larger debut was the new Insidious sequel, which opened in sixth with ₩974 million from 93,922 admissions — a modest start for the long-running horror franchise, landing in a week when family animation crowded the schedule and one blockbuster absorbed most of the adult audience. The Arrietty re-release was the other newcomer.

Movement elsewhere was limited: one film rose in the rankings while three fell. Hope, the Korean drama sitting eighth, illustrates the long tail of the current market — its ₩423 million week was small, but its cumulative audience of 4.52 million marks it as one of the year's substantial performers now in the final stretch of its run.

## The Shape of Late Summer

A 14.8% weekly revenue decline in late August fits the seasonal pattern of a market exhaling after its summer peak, but the composition of the chart is the more telling detail. With one Hollywood tentpole generating roughly two-thirds of the leaders' revenue and the rest of the top ten split among children's animation, franchise horror, and holdover Korean titles, exhibitors head into September with little depth beneath the front-runner. How quickly the chart refreshes once The Odyssey's run matures will determine whether the autumn corridor starts from strength or from a vacuum.
