Classical music took seven of the 50 slots on Korea's weekly performance box-office chart in week 31 of 2026, lifting the genre's share from 4% to 14% — a 10-percentage-point jump that made it the week's clearest mover, according to data from KOPIS, the national performing-arts box-office tracker. The gain came alongside a broader loosening at the top of the market: the leading genre's share of the chart slipped from 46% to 42%, and 30 productions entered the ranking for the first time.

## A Tenfold Week for the Concert Hall

The classical surge was built on breadth rather than a single blockbuster. Seven classical productions made the top 50, but only one — the Vienna Philharmonic's Seoul concert, at No. 10 — reached the top of the chart. That pattern suggests a genre filling out the middle of the ranking, more than tripling its footprint from the prior week's 4% share.

## Pop Concerts Still Own the Summit

Even in a week defined by classical's climb, popular music remained the chart's largest genre with 21 of the 50 entries, and it held the top two positions outright. YUURI LIVE led the ranking, followed by the DINO-fronted "吉BOARD SHOW" in Seoul at No. 2. DONGHAE's first solo encore concert, "ALIVE," landed at No. 4, and Jay Park's world-tour replay stop in Seoul took No. 6. The 42% concentration figure — down four points on the week — belongs to this genre, meaning pop's grip loosened even as it kept the podium.

## Musicals Anchor the Middle of the Chart

Musicals supplied 16 of the 50 entries, the second-largest bloc, and dominated positions three through nine. "Elisabeth" was the week's top musical at No. 3, with "Dracula" at No. 5, "Beethoven" at No. 7, "Hell's Kitchen" at No. 8, and the Daehangno production "Western Story" at No. 9. Theater added four entries, while dance and circus/magic contributed one apiece — a reminder of how thin the chart runs beyond the big three genres.

## High Turnover Beneath the Surface

The week's churn was substantial: 30 new productions entered the top 50, while seven titles climbed the ranking and eleven fell. Taken together with the four-point drop in top-genre concentration, the numbers describe a market distributing audiences across more productions and more genres than the week before. Whether classical music can hold a double-digit share once the Vienna Philharmonic's visit passes through the chart is the number to check in next week's data.
