Kakao Entertainment and Walt Disney Company Korea have entered a strategic partnership aimed at taking K-pop intellectual property to wider global audiences and developing crossover opportunities with Disney properties. The agreement brings together Kakao Entertainment’s music business and artist-related content with Disney’s internationally recognized portfolio, although specific projects and commercial terms have not been disclosed.

## A broader canvas for K-pop

The partnership points to an expansion of K-pop beyond recorded music and concerts. Combining artists, characters and stories could support content and merchandise designed for audiences already familiar with both Korean pop culture and Disney franchises.

For Kakao Entertainment, the arrangement offers a potential route into Disney’s global consumer ecosystem. For Disney Korea, it creates another way to engage K-pop audiences through locally developed concepts rather than relying solely on imported franchises.

The announcement does not yet identify participating artists or Disney properties. It also leaves open whether the first collaborations will focus on music, video, live events, merchandise or a combination of formats. Those details will determine whether the alliance produces one-off promotional projects or longer-running franchises.

## Disney’s established Korean-content footprint

Disney already has experience distributing major Korean productions internationally through Disney+. The South Korean mystery thriller *Nine Puzzles*, for example, ran for 11 episodes and was released from May 21 through June 4, 2025.

Its rollout was structured in three batches: six episodes arrived on May 21, followed by three on May 28 and two on June 4. The series had a reported production budget of 30 billion won, illustrating the scale of investment that can accompany premium Korean content.

Written by Lee Eun-mi and directed by Yoon Jong-bin and Kim Jung-ho, *Nine Puzzles* stars Kim Da-mi as Yoon E-na and Son Suk-ku as Kim Han-saem. The drama is not presented as part of the new Kakao Entertainment agreement, but its release demonstrates Disney’s existing role in bringing Korean creative properties to viewers beyond the domestic market.

## Execution will define the partnership

The central question is how the companies will turn two powerful pools of intellectual property into products that feel coherent rather than promotional. Artist selection, creative control and international distribution will be especially important because K-pop fandoms place considerable value on authenticity and continuity.

For now, the agreement establishes a framework rather than a finished slate. Its significance will become clearer when Kakao Entertainment and Disney Korea reveal the first participating acts, franchises and release plans.
