Min Hee-jin has won the damages lawsuit filed over her 2024 claim that the girl group ILLIT copied NewJeans, with the ruling now final. The court's reasoning, as reported on Friday, was that her remarks did not identify a specific victim, which meant the legal threshold for damages was not met.

## Why the Court Sided With Min

The suit centered on statements Min made during the public dispute between her, then chief executive of the NewJeans label ADOR, and parent company HYBE. In that dispute she argued that ILLIT, a group under HYBE's affiliate Belift Lab, had imitated NewJeans in concept and presentation.

The plaintiffs sought compensation on the ground that those remarks caused reputational and commercial harm. According to the reports, the court concluded that the statements were not directed at a legally identifiable person or entity in a way that would support a damages award. Under Korean defamation and tort law, a claim generally requires that the allegedly injured party be specifically identifiable from the statement itself. The court found that this condition was not satisfied here.

The word "final" in the reporting indicates that the judgment is no longer subject to appeal, closing this particular strand of litigation.

## A Long-Running Corporate Feud Reaches One Endpoint

The ILLIT comparison was one of the earliest and most public flashpoints in the 2024 conflict between Min and HYBE. That conflict escalated into board disputes at ADOR, a court fight over Min's position as chief executive, and eventually NewJeans' own attempt to exit their contracts. Several of those proceedings have unfolded separately from this damages case.

This ruling addresses only the question of whether Min owed compensation for the copying claim. It does not decide whether ILLIT did or did not copy NewJeans, and no court has made that determination. The judgment turns on the legal structure of the statement rather than on the artistic merits of either group.

## What This Means for the Two Groups and Their Labels

For ILLIT and Belift Lab, the outcome removes a possible avenue for recovering damages tied to the 2024 remarks, though it leaves their public position on the comparison unchanged. For Min, the win strengthens her legal footing at a time when her other disputes with HYBE remain active or have produced mixed results.

More broadly, the decision offers a reference point for how Korean courts treat sharp public commentary between entertainment executives. Criticism that is framed around concepts, styling, or group positioning, rather than pointed at a named individual, appears to face a high bar before it can support a damages claim.

Details of the damages sought and of any lower-court rulings were not included in the reports available for this article, and the reasoning summarized here reflects only what has been publicly reported.
