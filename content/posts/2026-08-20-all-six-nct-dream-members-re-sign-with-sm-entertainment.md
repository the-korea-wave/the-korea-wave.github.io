All six members of NCT Dream have renewed their exclusive contracts with SM Entertainment, the agency confirmed, keeping the group's lineup intact and removing one of the larger question marks hanging over the company's artist roster. In announcing the decision, the members characterized the agency as a dependable partner they intend to keep working alongside.

## A Clean Sweep, Not a Partial One

Contract renewals in K-pop rarely arrive this tidy. The industry's recent history is littered with split decisions — some members re-signing while others depart for solo management or rival labels, forcing groups into awkward part-time configurations. NCT Dream's renewal covers the entire lineup, which matters commercially: a full roster preserves the group's touring capacity, brand endorsement value, and album cadence without the discounting effect that lineup uncertainty imposes on advertisers and promoters.

The announcement was framed as official by the agency rather than surfacing through anonymous industry chatter, which removes the speculation window that typically depresses an agency's stock in the run-up to contract expirations.

## The Stakes for a ₩812 Billion Business

SM Entertainment reported revenue of roughly 812.5 billion won for fiscal 2025, with net income of about 124.6 billion won. For a company of that scale — 765 employees as of 2025, headquartered in Seoul's Seongsu-dong — retaining a top-line group is not a rounding error. Boy groups of NCT Dream's tier drive the album, concert, and merchandise lines that make up the bulk of a K-pop agency's income, and losing even one member typically forces a costly repositioning of the remaining act.

The company has been through this cycle before. Founded by Lee Soo-man as SM Studio on February 14, 1989, and reorganized under its current name in 1995, SM has repeatedly weathered contract disputes and departures across three decades of idol groups. A full renewal is the outcome the agency's playbook is built to produce, and one it has not always achieved.

## Why Shareholders Were Watching

The renewal lands in a distinct ownership era. Kakao and Kakao Entertainment together hold more than 41 percent of SM's shares — 21.61 percent and 19.89 percent respectively — with Tencent Music holding a further 9.66 percent. For a KOSDAQ-listed company (ticker 041510) whose value rests substantially on intangible artist contracts, roster stability is among the few concrete signals available to institutional owners assessing whether the post-acquisition SM can hold its talent.

That context makes this renewal less a routine personnel note than an early data point on whether SM under Kakao's stewardship can keep its core acts in-house. On this occasion, the answer was yes — unanimously.
