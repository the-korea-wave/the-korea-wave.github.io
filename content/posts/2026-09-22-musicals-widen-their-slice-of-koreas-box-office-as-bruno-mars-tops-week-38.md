Musicals took a markedly larger share of Korea's live-performance box office in week 38 of 2026, climbing from 22% to 30% of the tracked market, an 8 percentage-point gain in a single week, according to the Korea Performing Arts Box Office Information System (KOPIS). The shift came in a week dominated at the very top by concerts: Bruno Mars's Korea dates, Cho Yong-pil's national tour stop in Seoul, and Roy Kim's live tour filled the first three positions in the ranking of 50 productions.

## Concerts Hold the Peak, Musicals Fill the Middle

The two headline numbers pull in different directions, and both are true. Popular-music concerts accounted for 31 of the 50 ranked productions and nine of the top ten. Yet the musical genre's overall share of the box office expanded by 8 points. The reconciliation lies in ticket volume rather than chart position: 15 musicals sat in the ranking, and their combined sales grew faster than the market as a whole even without a musical breaking into the top three.

The remaining slots were thin. Circus and magic contributed 2 productions, Western classical music 1, and spoken theater 1. For the week, Korea's ranked performance market was almost entirely a two-genre affair.

## Top-Genre Concentration Tightens to 62%

The share held by the leading genre rose from 58% to 62%. That is a 4-point increase in concentration at the same time musicals gained ground, which means the squeeze fell on the smaller categories. Classical, theater, and variety acts together held only 4 of the 50 rankings, and their collective weight shrank further.

For a market often described as diversifying, the week's data reads the opposite way. A handful of large-venue concerts and long-running musicals absorbed the growth, and the long tail got shorter.

## Thirty-Nine New Entries, Ten Fallers

Turnover was unusually high. Thirty-nine productions entered the ranking for the first time this week, and the new arrivals included the three chart-toppers: Bruno Mars's Korea performance, Cho Yong-pil & Wi Dae Han Tan Saeng's Seoul tour concert, and Roy Kim's "R: O: Y in SEOUL." Only one existing production moved up the chart, while ten slid downward. With so many fresh entrants, most incumbents had nowhere to go but down.

## Two Musicals Break the Concert Wall

Cats, playing its international tour engagement in Daegu, reached fourth place, and Dracula in Seoul took sixth. They were the only non-concert productions in the top ten. Cats in particular is notable for landing that high from a regional city rather than the capital, at a time when Seoul and the surrounding Gyeonggi area hosted seven of the ten leaders.

Geographically, the top ten spread across Gyeonggi, Seoul, Daegu, Incheon, and Gwangju. Seoul alone held five positions. Gyeonggi hosted the two largest-arena events, the Bruno Mars and Doh Kyung-soo shows, which reflects where Korea's biggest indoor venues sit rather than where audiences live.

## What the Numbers Do Not Say

KOPIS ranking data measures box-office position, not revenue in won, and the genre shares reported here are shares of the tracked market rather than of all live performance in Korea. The week-over-week comparison covers a single seven-day window. A jump of 8 points in the musical share can reverse just as quickly when a major concert run ends and a touring production opens, so the more durable indicator is the concentration figure, which has been moving in one direction.
