Stray Kids have opened at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 with their latest album, extending their run of consecutive chart-topping entries to nine — a streak that, among groups, now trails only the Beatles in the chart's history. Every album the eight-member act has sent to the U.S. albums chart since 2022 has debuted at the top, an unbroken record no other K-pop act has matched.

## A Streak With Only One Precedent

The Billboard 200 has ranked America's best-performing albums for decades, and sustained dominance of its summit has been rare even for the industry's biggest names. Nine consecutive No. 1 albums places Stray Kids in territory previously occupied by the Beatles alone among groups — a comparison the Korean press was quick to draw when the chart result landed.

What makes the run notable is not any single week's performance but its consistency. Since their first No. 1 with "ODDINARY" in 2022, the group has converted every subsequent release into a chart-topping debut, spanning full albums, mini albums, and mixtape-format projects. That reliability has turned each release into a predictable commercial event for JYP Entertainment, the group's label, at a time when first-week performance increasingly defines a K-pop act's global standing.

## "We Will Keep Widening the Spectrum"

The members framed the achievement as a statement about the genre as much as themselves. In remarks released after the chart result, the group said they intend to show the world "the endless growth of K-pop" and pledged to keep broadening their musical range rather than settling into a proven formula.

That framing matters commercially. Stray Kids built their U.S. audience largely on a self-produced, harder-edged sound distinct from their contemporaries, and the group's stated intent to widen its spectrum suggests the next phase of the streak will be tested against new stylistic ground, not repetition of what has already worked.

## The Stakes for K-pop's Next Chapter

The milestone arrives as the Korean music industry leans harder on a small number of proven global acts. With several first-generation global groups in transition, Stray Kids' streak has become one of the industry's clearest ongoing claims to U.S. mainstream relevance — and one of the few active records with a realistic path toward an all-time mark.

Whether the run reaches ten will depend on the same mechanics that produced the first nine: a fan commerce structure built around physical albums, a touring footprint that keeps the group visible in North America, and a release cadence that has rarely let more than a year pass between projects. For now, the group holds a distinction that only the Beatles have surpassed — and the gap between them is narrowing with every release.
