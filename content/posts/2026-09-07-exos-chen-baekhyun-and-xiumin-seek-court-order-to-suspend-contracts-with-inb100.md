EXO members Chen, Baekhyun and Xiumin have filed for a court injunction to suspend the validity of their exclusive contracts with INB100, the agency that has represented the trio in recent years. The filing means the three singers, active together as the unit EXO-CBX, are now in a legal dispute with their own management company — the second time the group's members have turned to contract litigation after their high-profile standoff with SM Entertainment in 2023.

## A Second Front in the Trio's Contract Battles

An application for suspension of contract validity — a 가처분, or preliminary injunction, in Korean civil procedure — asks a court to freeze the legal effect of an exclusive contract before any full lawsuit is decided. If granted, the artists would be released from their obligations to the agency while the underlying dispute plays out, effectively allowing them to work independently in the interim.

That the counterparty this time is INB100 is the striking element. The trio moved under the INB100 banner after their dispute with SM Entertainment, which centered on the terms and duration of their original exclusive contracts, ended in a settlement. Filing against the very agency that emerged from that episode suggests the arrangement that was supposed to stabilize their careers has itself broken down, though the specific grievances behind the new application have not been detailed in the initial reports.

## What Suspension Would Mean in Practice

Korean courts have handled a series of similar injunctions in the entertainment industry, and the pattern is consistent: a granted suspension does not by itself void a contract, but it strips the agency of its ability to enforce exclusivity — scheduling, appearance approvals, revenue collection — until a main judgment or settlement resolves the relationship. For working artists, that interim freedom is often the real prize, which is why the injunction stage frequently determines the practical outcome long before any final ruling.

For Chen, Baekhyun and Xiumin, a favorable decision would put their management status in flux for the third time since their 2012 debut under SM Entertainment: first as SM artists, then under INB100, and potentially next as free agents or under a yet-unknown structure.

## The Stakes Beyond One Group

The case lands in an industry still recalibrating after several years of artist-agency litigation, in which courts have increasingly scrutinized long exclusive terms and opaque settlement structures. Each new injunction filing by established artists adds to the body of precedent that younger acts and their lawyers study closely. If members of one of K-pop's most commercially successful groups can twice invoke the courts against successive agencies, the leverage balance between artists and management companies shifts a little further — regardless of how this particular application is decided.

Key details remain unconfirmed at this stage, including the grounds cited in the filing, INB100's formal response, and the timetable for a court decision. Those will determine whether this becomes a short-lived negotiation tactic or a full separation.
