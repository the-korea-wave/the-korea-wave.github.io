The Seoul Central District Court has granted EXO-CBX an injunction suspending the effect of the group’s exclusive contracts with INB100. The ruling gives Chen, Baekhyun and Xiumin interim relief after unpaid settlement money was found to have undermined the relationship of trust with their agency.

## Why the injunction matters

The decision prevents INB100 from enforcing the exclusive agreements while the underlying dispute proceeds. It is an interim court measure rather than a final judgment resolving every contractual and financial claim between the parties.

For the three EXO members, however, the immediate consequence is significant: their obligation to remain bound by the disputed contracts has been put on hold. The court’s focus on unpaid settlements also places INB100’s accounting and payment obligations at the center of the case.

## INB100’s place in a larger label network

INB100 joined One Hundred Label’s multi-label operation in 2024. One Hundred Label had been launched by Cha Ga-won and her partners in 2023, when it also added Big Planet Made Entertainment and Million Market to its holdings.

Cha entered the entertainment industry after primarily working in property and has since become associated with an expanding portfolio of music companies. Her separate financial dealings have also drawn attention: she filed a civil suit seeking repayment of 12 billion won in loans from MC Mong. That case is distinct from the EXO-CBX contract dispute.

## A dispute now centered on agency accountability

The injunction shifts the immediate balance toward Chen, Baekhyun and Xiumin, but it does not close the broader legal conflict. Questions concerning unpaid amounts, contractual responsibility and the parties’ future business arrangements may still require further litigation or a negotiated settlement.

The ruling nevertheless establishes a clear near-term position: INB100 cannot rely on the suspended exclusive contracts as though they remained fully enforceable.
