The Recording Academy has indicated it may rework the Best Asian Pop Music Performance category, roughly two weeks after BTS declared a boycott of the Grammy Awards over the newly created field. In a statement addressing the standoff, Grammy chief executive Harvey Mason Jr. said the Academy has recently been in conversations with Hybe, the group's parent company, along with other industry figures — the first concrete indication that the organization is willing to reopen a decision that provoked the most prominent act in K-pop to walk away.

## A Boycott That Forced a Statement

The sequence matters. BTS announced its boycott first; the Academy's response came about fifteen days later, and it arrived not as a quiet procedural note but as a statement from the CEO acknowledging direct dialogue with the company behind the group. Mason stopped short of committing to scrap the category, framing the Academy's position as openness to modifying it. That distinction — retooling rather than retreating outright — is why Korean coverage has framed the move as a question ("a white flag?") rather than a settled outcome.

## Recognition or Relegation

The dispute turns on a long-running tension in awards politics: whether a dedicated regional or genre category honors artists or fences them off from the marquee fields. For an act of BTS's standing — the group debuted under Big Hit on June 13, 2013, and went on to define K-pop's global expansion — an "Asian pop" lane can read less like recognition and more like a ceiling. Refusing to participate at all is the strongest lever an artist has against an award show, and it is one very few acts could pull without simply disappearing from the conversation. BTS evidently could.

## The Company Across the Table

The negotiating partner the Academy named is not a boutique label. Hybe, founded by Bang Si-hyuk in February 2005 as Big Hit Entertainment and renamed on March 30, 2021, posted revenue of roughly 2.658 trillion won in fiscal 2025, though net income fell to about 254.4 billion won. The Yongsan-based company trades on the Korea Exchange under code 352820 and employed 893 people as of 2026, with Bang remaining the largest shareholder at 28.4 percent, ahead of the National Pension Service at 8.2 percent. When Mason says the Academy has been talking with Hybe, he is describing a negotiation between the American recording industry's awards body and the corporate center of gravity of Korean pop — not a courtesy call to an aggrieved artist.

## What Is Actually on Offer

For now, the Academy has signaled willingness, not action. There is no confirmed replacement structure, no stated timeline, and no word on whether a revised category — or its elimination — would be enough to bring BTS back into the process. The boycott achieved in two weeks what public criticism of category design rarely achieves at all: a CEO-level acknowledgment that the field may change. Whether that becomes a genuine restructuring or a cosmetic rename is the question the next Academy announcement will have to answer.
