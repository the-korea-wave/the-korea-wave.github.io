Pop music tightened its grip on Korea's live-performance market in the first week of September, taking 27 of the 50 spots on the national box office ranking — a 54% share, up eight percentage points from 46% the week before, according to data from the Korea Performing Arts Box Office Information System (KOPIS).

## A Concert-Led Reshuffle at the Top

The genre's advance was powered by fresh openings rather than holdovers. Twenty-eight productions entered the top 50 during the week, and three of the most prominent newcomers were concerts: singer-songwriter Kim Dong-ryul's "Old Songs," which debuted at No. 1; the WONDERLIVET festival in Gyeonggi Province at No. 2; and Choi Yu-ree's "Meomureum" in Seoul at No. 5. Choi's Busan run of the same tour landed one rung lower at No. 6, giving the indie vocalist two entries in the top ten in a single week.

The rest of the upper table shows how thoroughly concerts now share space with big-brand musicals. "Dracula" in Seoul held No. 3 and "Dear Evan Hansen" No. 4, with "Frozen" at No. 8 — the only three musicals in the top ten. The Weeknd's appearance at Hyundai Card Super Concert 28 in Gyeonggi Province ranked No. 7, singer So Su-bin's solo show in Seoul took No. 9, and an orchestral program tied to the game Trickcal Revive rounded out the list at No. 10, the lone classical entry that high on the chart. Hyundai Card, the credit card issuer formed when Hyundai Motor Group acquired and rebranded Diners Club Korea, has made the Super Concert series a fixture of large-scale touring stops in Korea.

## Musicals Hold Volume, Not Momentum

By count, musicals remained the second-largest bloc with 17 of the 50 ranked productions, followed by circus and magic with three, Western classical music with two, and straight theater with just one. But the week's churn favored concerts: 10 productions climbed the ranking while 11 fell, and the eight-point swing toward pop music came almost entirely at the expense of everything else's share of the table.

The concentration figure tells the same story from another angle. The leading genre's share of the top 50 rose from 46% to 54% week-on-week, meaning the market's single biggest category now accounts for more than half of all ranked productions — a level of dominance that typically appears during festival season and major tour windows, when arena and stadium dates crowd out longer-running theatrical fare.

## Why the Balance Is Tilting

The pattern in the data points to supply as the driver. A 28-production intake in one week is a heavy rotation for a 50-slot chart, and when that intake skews toward one-off and short-run concerts, the ranking naturally tilts toward pop music even if musical attendance holds steady. Musicals like "Dracula" and "Dear Evan Hansen" run for weeks or months and accumulate sales continuously; concerts spike, chart, and exit. A week in which Kim Dong-ryul, a major international act, a festival, and a two-city indie tour all go on sale or on stage at once will compress the musicals' presence regardless of their underlying health.

The question for the coming weeks is whether the 54% share marks a seasonal peak or a new baseline. With autumn touring schedules filling out and year-end musical season still ahead, the two blocs are set to keep trading points — but for now, the concert stage is where Korean audiences are spending.
