Choi Min-sik and Han So-hee’s “Intern” has moved into first place at the Korean box office, dislodging “Odyssey” and returning a Korean production to the top of the chart after an extended absence.

## Star pairing delivers an immediate breakthrough

The result gives “Intern” a clear early marker of audience interest. Choi brings the recognition of one of Korean cinema’s most established actors, while Han adds a younger following built across film, television and streaming releases.

That combination provides the movie with broad promotional reach, but its chart position is the more concrete test: “Intern” has converted public attention into enough admissions to overtake the incumbent leader.

## A lift for homegrown cinema

The change at the top also matters beyond a single release. A Korean film had not held the leading box-office position for weeks, leaving imported titles to dominate the market.

“Intern” has interrupted that run. Its ascent could strengthen exhibitors’ confidence in domestic releases and give the film more visibility through prominent scheduling, although its staying power will depend on audience response and competition from upcoming titles.

## Longevity is now the key test

Reaching first place establishes momentum, but it does not yet show whether “Intern” can sustain it. Weekend attendance, word of mouth and the arrival of new releases will determine whether the film develops into a durable theatrical hit or records a shorter spell at the summit.

For now, the headline is unambiguous: “Intern” has beaten “Odyssey” to become Korea’s box-office leader.
