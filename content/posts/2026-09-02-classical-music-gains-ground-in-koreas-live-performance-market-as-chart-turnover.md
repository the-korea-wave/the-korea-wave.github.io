Classical music expanded its foothold in South Korea's live performance market in the last week of August, with the genre's share of the box office top 50 rising from 4% to 6% — a two-percentage-point gain in a chart that remains dominated by pop concerts and musicals. The shift came during a week of unusually high turnover: 34 productions entered the ranking for the first time, while 11 titles slid down the chart against only four that climbed.

## A Crowded Week at the Top

The top of the chart belonged to K-pop. EXO's arena production *EXO PLANET #6, EXhOrizon [dot]* in Seoul took first place, and AKMU's *Paradise of Rumors* concert, also in Seoul, landed at fourth — both among the week's 34 new entries. Pop music accounted for 24 of the 50 ranked productions, nearly half the chart.

Musicals filled much of the remaining space with 16 entries, led by two productions of *Dracula* running simultaneously in Daegu and Seoul at second and third place. *Elisabeth* (fifth), *Frozen* (sixth), and the Daehangno productions *Western Story* (ninth) and *Gone Tomorrow* (tenth) rounded out the genre's presence in the top 10, alongside solo concerts by Redoor (seventh) and trot singer Young Tak in Jeonju (eighth).

## Classical's Quiet Climb

Against that backdrop, classical music's move from two chart entries to three is modest in absolute terms but notable as a share shift. Western classical repertoire held 6% of the top 50, up from 4% the week before. Theater matched classical with three entries, while dance and circus/magic productions held two apiece.

The genre's gain coincided with a slight loosening of the market's concentration: the combined share of the leading genres fell from 50% to 48%. In a chart where pop music and musicals together account for 40 of 50 slots, even small movements in the remaining tail change the texture of what audiences can find in the ranking.

## Turnover as the Week's Defining Feature

The more telling number may be churn. With 34 of 50 positions occupied by newcomers, more than two-thirds of the chart turned over in a single week — a pattern driven by the concert calendar, where major pop acts open and close runs quickly, in contrast to musicals' long engagements. The imbalance between risers (4) and fallers (11) among returning titles reflects that squeeze: incumbent productions largely gave ground as the new wave of concerts arrived.

Whether classical music holds its expanded share will depend on how the genre's typically shorter engagement cycles interact with that churn. For now, the week's data points to a market that remains concert- and musical-led, but marginally less so than before.
