Musicals expanded their footprint in South Korea's live-performance market in the third week of August, climbing from 32% to 36% of the national box-office ranking — a gain of four percentage points — even as the market's single largest genre loosened its grip. Data from the Korea Performing Arts Box Office Information System (KOPIS) for the week show top-genre concentration easing from 48% to 42%, pointing to a ranking that is spreading across more categories rather than consolidating.

## A 50-Title Ranking Tilts Toward the Stage

The weekly ranking covered 50 productions. Pop-music concerts remained the largest single category at 21 entries, but musicals closed the gap with 18 — the source of that 36% share. Theater followed with 6 titles, Western classical music with 3, and dance and circus/magic with one apiece.

The shift was a two-sided move: musicals added share at the same time the leading genre's concentration dropped six percentage points. In practical terms, the concert-driven top of the market thinned slightly, and mid-ranking slots absorbed more stage productions. Turnover underneath the headline numbers was brisk but balanced — 9 productions moved up the ranking while 10 fell.

## Big Concerts Still Own the Summit

Genre share is one story; the very top of the chart is another. BIGBANG's world-tour stop in Goyang, Gyeonggi Province, held the number-one position, and pop acts took six of the top ten slots overall. Park Ji Hoon's Seoul encore fancon "RE: FLECT" ranked third, the TVT (The Volunteers) concert placed sixth, tripleS's Seoul date on its "ANDLESS" world tour came in eighth, Christopher's "SJF at the Theater" ninth, and CLICK-B's Busan encore show tenth — the lone top-ten entry staged outside the capital region alongside the Goyang opener.

Musicals supplied the other four: "Frozen" in Seoul at number two, "Elisabeth" fourth, "Dracula" fifth, and "Hell's Kitchen" seventh. All four ran in Seoul, underlining how concentrated the musical business remains in the capital even as the genre's overall share grows.

## Fresh Inventory Behind the Churn

Thirty productions entered the ranking for the first time during the week — a substantial injection of new inventory into a 50-title chart. Notably, several debutants landed high immediately: Park Ji Hoon's encore fancon, the TVT concert, and tripleS's Seoul tour date all entered directly into the top ten, a pattern typical of short-run idol concerts that sell out on announcement and then exit the chart just as quickly.

That dynamic frames the musical genre's gains. Concert entries spike and vanish with tour schedules, while long-running musicals such as "Frozen," "Elisabeth," and "Dracula" accumulate week after week of ranked performance. If the summer concert calendar thins into September, the structural advantage of open-ended musical runs suggests the genre's share has room to hold — or extend — its current level.
