Stray Kids have crossed the three-million mark in first-week album sales for a fourth time. JYP Entertainment said on August 15 that the group's mini album *THIS & THAT*, released on August 7, sold 3,289,269 copies in the seven days following release — the window the Korean industry tracks as "chodong," or first-week sales — making it the group's fourth consecutive release to clear three million in that period.

## A Fourth Entry in a Rarefied Club

Triple million status remains one of the most exclusive benchmarks in the Korean physical album market. First-week sales are counted from the release date through day seven, and the figure functions as the industry's headline measure of a fanbase's purchasing power, since it captures pre-orders and launch-week demand before streaming momentum or chart placements enter the picture.

For Stray Kids, the milestone is less a breakout than a confirmation of an established floor. Repeating a three-million opening across four releases indicates that the group's buying base has held at scale rather than spiking on a single title — a distinction that matters commercially, because labels and distributors plan pressing runs, retail allocations and version lineups around the expected floor, not the one-off peak.

## What the Number Means for JYP

Physical albums remain a core revenue line for K-pop's major agencies, and a dependable three-million opening gives JYP Entertainment a rare degree of predictability in a market where most acts see first-week totals swing widely between comebacks. An August 7 release also positions *THIS & THAT* to anchor the label's third-quarter results, with launch-week volume booked almost entirely inside the quarter.

The result lands at a moment when the broader industry has been debating whether top-tier physical sales can keep expanding as export mixes shift and fan-purchase patterns evolve. A fourth triple million opening from a single act does not settle that question, but it does show that the ceiling for K-pop's biggest sellers has not come down.

## The Bar the Group Has Set for Itself

The flip side of a consistent three-million floor is that it becomes the expectation. Each subsequent comeback will now be read against the 3.29 million figure *THIS & THAT* posted, and any meaningful shortfall would be treated as a signal of cooling demand even at levels most groups never approach. For now, the August numbers put Stray Kids in the small tier of acts whose physical launches are measured in millions per week — and extend a streak that has become one of the more reliable fixtures in the Korean album market.
