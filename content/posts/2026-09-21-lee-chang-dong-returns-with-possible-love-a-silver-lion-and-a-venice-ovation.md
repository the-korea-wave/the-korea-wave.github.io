Lee Chang-dong's "Possible Love" has returned to Korea with a Silver Lion from the Venice Film Festival, the director's first competition prize there and his first feature in the eight years since "Burning." The film's world premiere at the Lido ended with a standing ovation that ran about seven minutes, and it has since added an Auteur Award from the Mill Valley Film Festival in California. For a filmmaker who has released only a handful of features across three decades, the double recognition marks one of the more consequential returns in recent Korean cinema.

## Eight years between films, and what changed

Lee has said the gap since "Burning," which competed at Cannes in 2018, gave him a different vantage point on Venice this time. Speaking to the Korean press after the premiere, he described the award as gratifying and an honor, while also arguing that the prize itself was not the point. What mattered more to him, by his own account, was whether the film's questions reached the audience.

That framing is consistent with how Lee has talked about his work for years. His films tend to withhold resolution and leave viewers to sit with moral and emotional ambiguity, and Korean critics writing about "Possible Love" have again described it as a film that raises more questions than it answers.

## A reunion with Jeon Do-yeon

The film also reunites Lee with Jeon Do-yeon, who won the Best Actress prize at Cannes in 2007 for his "Secret Sunshine." Jeon told reporters that working with Lee again after that project felt comfortable and even restorative, a notable comment given the emotional intensity "Secret Sunshine" was known for.

For Korean audiences, the pairing carries weight. "Secret Sunshine" remains one of the defining performances of Jeon's career, and the collaboration was long cited as an example of how Lee draws demanding work from actors.

## Two festivals, two prizes

The Venice honor was followed by the Mill Valley Film Festival, which gave Lee its Auteur Award. Mill Valley is a smaller event than Venice but has a track record of programming films that go on to compete in the North American awards season, and the recognition extends the film's visibility in the United States as it prepares for its Korean release.

The exact category of the Silver Lion has not been detailed in the Korean coverage, and the film's domestic release date has not been confirmed in the reports available. Both are worth confirming from the distributor before drawing conclusions about the film's commercial rollout.

## Why this return matters beyond one film

Lee is one of the few Korean directors whose work is routinely placed in competition at the major European festivals, and his output is sparse enough that each new film functions as an event. "Burning" was Korea's submission for the Academy Awards and made the shortlist, and it remains a reference point for international critics writing about Korean cinema outside the commercial genre space.

"Possible Love" arrives at a moment when Korean film's international profile is often measured by streaming hits and genre exports. A Silver Lion for a quiet, question-driven drama from a director in his seventies is a reminder that the art-house strand of Korean cinema still carries its own weight abroad, independent of the industry's more commercial success stories.
