SM Entertainment grew both its top and bottom line in the second quarter of 2026, reporting consolidated revenue of 349.6 billion won and operating profit of 52.9 billion won in results released on August 5 — increases of 15.4 percent and 11.0 percent, respectively, from the same period a year earlier. The company attributed the gains to heavy global concert touring by its artists, expansion in merchandise and licensing, and improved performance at its subsidiaries.

## Touring and Licensing Carry the Quarter

The earnings underline how far the revenue mix at Korea's major music companies has shifted toward live performance and intellectual-property businesses. SM pointed to its artists' international tour schedules as a primary growth driver for the quarter, alongside merchandise and licensing operations that monetize artist IP well beyond album sales. Subsidiary results also improved, contributing to the consolidated figures.

Operating profit growing more slowly than revenue — 11.0 percent against 15.4 percent — is consistent with the cost profile of a touring-heavy quarter, where production and logistics expenses scale with activity, though the company still converted roughly 15 percent of revenue into operating profit.

## A New Girls' Generation Unit Headlines the Second Half

Alongside the results, SM announced that Hyorisu, a new unit drawn from Girls' Generation, will release a single in the third quarter. The move extends a familiar SM playbook: reactivating one of its longest-running franchises through smaller unit configurations that can record and promote on tighter timelines than a full group reunion, keeping a legacy IP commercially active between larger projects.

## The Roster Keeps Widening Beyond Idol Groups

The touring-and-licensing engine sits on top of a roster strategy that has been broadening for two years. In May 2024, SM launched Krucialize, an R&B-focused imprint, and signed singer-songwriter Min Jiwoon as its first artist. She debuted that October with "Sentimental Love" and released her first EP, "Pink, then grey" — six songs she wrote and composed herself — in August 2025, including a collaboration featuring vocalist Crush among her standalone singles.

That imprint remains small relative to SM's idol business, but it illustrates the direction of the company's IP portfolio: more artist types, more release formats, and more surfaces on which to build licensing and touring revenue. With a Girls' Generation unit single slated for the third quarter and its artists continuing international tour runs, SM enters the second half with the same drivers that produced this quarter's double-digit growth still in motion.
