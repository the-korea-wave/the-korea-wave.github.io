South Korean cinemas had a markedly quieter week in the last full week of July 2026, with total ticket revenue falling 44.5% from the previous week. The pullback came from a familiar pattern: the holdover titles that had powered the prior week cooled sharply, and the two films that opened fresh arrived far too small to make up the difference.

## One title carrying the week

The chart was defined by a single dominant release. "Hope" took first place with 13,526,394,400 won in weekly revenue and 1,286,068 admissions, lifting its running total to 3,415,704 admissions. To put that in perspective, no other film in the top ten cleared even two billion won for the week. With the top three titles accounting for 83.1% of all revenue — down only slightly from 83.8% a week earlier — and "Hope" alone representing roughly two-thirds of the entire market, the week's takings rose and fell almost entirely on one movie's momentum.

That concentration is a double-edged fact. It shows a genuine hit anchoring the box office, but it also means the overall 44.5% decline largely reflects how much thinner everything below the leader had become.

## The chase pack falls off quickly

Behind the leader, the drop-off was steep. "Minions & Monsters" held second place with 1,857,133,210 won and 188,277 admissions (562,965 cumulative), while "Moana" ranked third on 1,375,847,410 won and 137,181 admissions, pushing its total to 909,270. From there the numbers thin out fast: "Toy Story 5" was fourth with 882,857,510 won (86,956 admissions for the week, 2,837,123 in total), and "Nundongja" fifth with 841,203,260 won and 80,408 admissions against a 1,511,619 running total.

The mid-table titles tell the story of the week's softness. "David" sat sixth on 579,595,310 won, and "Gunche" — despite a large 5,949,976 cumulative audience built up over its run — brought in just 94,165,240 won for the week in eighth, a sign of a title deep in its tail.

## New arrivals open cold

Crucially for the weekly total, the two new releases entered at the bottom rather than the top. "Dream Animals: The Movie" debuted at seventh with 148,167,510 won and 16,703 admissions, while the other newcomer opened tenth on just 64,133,400 won and 6,862 admissions. Neither had the scale to offset the fading holdovers, which is why no film climbed in the rankings and two slipped.

## What the week signals for the weeks ahead

The core takeaway is that this was a market coasting on one film. With "Hope" already past 3.4 million cumulative admissions and its weekly haul certain to taper, and with the latest debuts landing well below the level needed to lead, the chart is short on a clear successor. Unless an incoming release opens at genuine blockbuster scale, the high single-title concentration seen here leaves the overall box office exposed to further week-on-week swings.
